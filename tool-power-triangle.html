What it means

kW is real power: what actually does work (heat, motion, light). kVAR is reactive power: what motors and transformers use to build their magnetic fields. kVA is what the wires and transformer have to carry, and it combines the two: kVA² = kW² + kVAR².

Power factor = kW ÷ kVA. A heater is about 1.0. A lightly loaded motor can be 0.8 or lower, which means more current for the same work. That’s why VA is often higher than watts on a nameplate.
